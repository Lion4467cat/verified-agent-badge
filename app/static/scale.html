UNCLONE — Scale & Viability

Who buys, why a user-side verifier beats the channel conflict, and how a pilot starts. Raw version: SCALE.md

What the bank wantsComplete the call — revenue and service cost — and stop the scam — loss and liability. Today both jobs sit with the human agent… which is exactly the thing being cloned.
vs
What the customer wantsTo trust the caller. Caller ID is spoofable, KYC answers leak, and "we never ask for X" buys nothing against a cloned agent that doesn't have to ask.
The resolution is structural, not a slogan: the verifier is the customer's control, not the contact centre's. It protects the one case the contact centre cannot see — a perfect clone of the agent's own voice — it produces the decision log regulators ask for (which window, which score, which action, when), and it works on calls the bank's infrastructure never touches.

Buyer

Fraud-ops + digital channels
Not the contact centre. The SDK ships where the step-up prompt already lives: the bank's app, first; CCaaS-side for banks without app reach. Telephony (L3) is declared future work.

Cost per caught fraud

≈ $42
Every flagged call is analysed at ≈ $0.45 (measured plan price × 3-window design). At 1.33 % incidence and 80 % detection, one catch costs ~94 analyses ≈ $42 — $60 per prevented fraud after the 70 % intervention step — against an $800 median loss (FTC 2024).

vs human review

>$11×
A human fraud review of a flagged call runs $5–15 (assumption) and cannot hear a clone of the agent's own voice; the machine layer costs ≈ $0.45 per call and catches that case.

Economy of scale

Linear, idle-free
Per-operation pricing: a bank pays only for flagged traffic it analyses. No standing cost, no cold-start corpus.

The enrolment runbook (the "where is your dataset?" answer, productised)

StepWhat actually happens
1 · SourceExisting compliance recordings of the specific agent roster assigned to payment-confirmation calls — 15–60 s of clean speech per agent. No new capture, no third-party corpus, no cold start.
2 · BasisThe recordings are already captured for regulatory retention; verification use runs on the same privacy basis. No biometric template is exported — the speaker template stays inside the vendor; we hold only ids and scores.
3 · StoreEnrolment via the vendor's speaker API. Our side keeps speaker_id + a content fingerprint. No raw audio retained.
4 · DeleteOn agent offboarding: one deletion call per speaker, evidenced in the decision log.
